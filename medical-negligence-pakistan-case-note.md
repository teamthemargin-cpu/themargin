## Summary

The case of Mariam Sajjad v. Prof. Dr. Rasool Ahmed Chaudhary brings to light a critical yet often overlooked issue in Pakistan's healthcare system — medical negligence as a violation of fundamental human rights. At its core, the case revolves around the responsibility of medical professionals to uphold a reasonable standard of care. When this duty is compromised, it is not merely a professional lapse — it directly threatens a patient's right to life and dignity. This case note argues that access to safe and competent medical care is not just a service but a basic human right, and that stronger legal safeguards and accountability are needed to protect patients from similar violations in the future.

## Academic abstract

This case note examines Mariam Sajjad v. Prof. Dr. Rasool Ahmed Chaudhary, adjudicated by the Lahore High Court on 19 December 2023, concerning allegations of medical negligence and the scope of professional liability in healthcare. The case raises critical questions regarding the standard of care owed by medical practitioners and the evidentiary threshold required to establish negligence in civil claims.

The Court evaluated whether the conduct of the respondent deviated from accepted medical standards and whether such deviation caused actionable harm to the patient. In doing so, it engaged with principles governing duty of care, breach, and causation within the Pakistani legal framework.

This note argues that the judgment reflects a cautious judicial approach in assessing medical liability, while also underscoring the need for stronger accountability mechanisms in the healthcare sector. The case is significant for its implications on the protection of patients' fundamental rights, particularly the right to life and dignity, and contributes to the evolving discourse on medical negligence in Pakistan.

## Key findings

In Mariam Sajjad v. Prof. Dr. Rasool Ahmed Chaudhary, the Court held that medical negligence cannot be presumed from an adverse medical outcome alone. Liability arises only when it is clearly proved that the doctor breached the standard of care and such breach directly caused harm. The Court emphasized the need for strong evidence and maintained a cautious approach to protect medical professionals from unfounded claims.
