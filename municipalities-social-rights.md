## Summary

Cities are often the first point of contact for people claiming their rights — yet municipalities frequently lack the legal powers and funding to protect those rights consistently. This research compares how Barcelona, Turin, and Athens handle the rights to education, health and housing, finding that local innovation matters but cannot substitute for clear legal competences and stable financing.

## Key findings

Local innovation and political will are real — but rights only become durable when backed by law, stable funding, and intergovernmental coordination. Even cities with limited formal powers find creative workarounds — but those workarounds remain fragile.
